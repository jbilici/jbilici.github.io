Butt-weld fitting take-out chart (ASME B16.9 elbows, tees, reducers)

Center-to-end take-outs for long-radius and short-radius 90° elbows, 45° elbows, tees and reducers, NPS ½" to 12", and how to use them for cut lengths.

The take-out is the distance from a fitting's center to its welding end. Subtract the take-outs and the weld gaps from the center-to-center distance to get the length of pipe to cut.

Values agree across Texas Flange, Wermac and the Weldbend catalog. Your fittings may differ slightly by maker: measure one if the fit matters.

Cut length

Cut = center to center − take-out₁ − take-out₂ − (gap × number of welds)

Example: two 3" LR 90° elbows, 40½" center to center, 1/8" root gap:

  • 40.5 − 4.50 − 4.50 − 2 × 0.125 = 31.25" (31 1/4")

A reducer in the run takes out its whole length: a 4" LR 90° into a 4 × 2 reducer, then 2" pipe to a 2" LR 90° at 30" center to center leaves 30 − 6.00 − 4.00 − 3.00 − 3 × 0.125 = 16.625" (16 5/8") of 2" pipe.

Watch out

  • Don't use the formula for 45° elbows. 1.5 × NPS × tan 22.5° gives 1.24" for 2", but the B16.9 table says 1.38". The table wins at every size.
  • LR 90° below 1" is not 1.5 × NPS. ½" and ¾" are both 1.50".
  • Short-radius elbows start at 1".
  • Socket-weld and threaded fittings use different dimensions (ASME B16.11) and different cut rules (socket pull-back, thread engagement).

For angled pipe between the fittings, start with the offset multipliers or the rolling offset guide.

Always verify measurements on the job before cutting.
