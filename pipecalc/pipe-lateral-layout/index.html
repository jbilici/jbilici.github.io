How to lay out a pipe lateral (45° branch cut and header hole)

The ordinate method for a lateral, a branch that meets the header at 45° or any angle: the formula, a full 16-station table for 4" on 6" at 45°, the header hole length and where the work point sits.

Updated October 18, 2026 · by Jiyan Bilici

A lateral is a branch that joins the header at an angle other than 90°, usually 45°. Like a saddle, you cut the branch end with ordinates measured at equal stations around the pipe, but the cut is lopsided: short on one side, long on the other.

Terms

  • Work point (WP): where the branch centerline crosses the header centerline. All lengths are measured from here.
  • Lateral angle α: the angle between the two centerlines (45° for a standard lateral).
  • Toe: the side of the branch on the acute side of the angle (the inside of the 45°). The branch is shortest here.
  • Heel: the opposite side, on the obtuse side. The branch is longest here.
  • R = header OD ÷ 2, r = branch OD ÷ 2, φ = station angle around the branch, counted from the toe (0° at the toe, 180° at the heel).

The formula

Distance along the branch centerline from the work point to the cut at station φ:

s(φ) = [√(R² − r² × sin²φ) + r × cos φ × cos α] ÷ sin α

  • Ordinate at each station = s(0) − s(φ). It is 0 at the toe and largest at or near the heel.
  • Hole length along the top of the header = branch OD ÷ sin α.
  • The branch centerline crosses the top of the header R ÷ tan α from the work point.
  • Station spacing on the wrap-around = π × branch OD ÷ number of stations.

Worked example: 4" on 6" at 45°

Branch 4" (OD 4.500, r = 2.250), header 6" (OD 6.625, R = 3.3125), α = 45°.

  • Toe, s(0) = (3.3125 + 2.250 × 0.7071) ÷ 0.7071 = 6.935" from the work point
  • Heel, s(180°) = (3.3125 − 2.250 × 0.7071) ÷ 0.7071 = 2.435" from the work point
  • Heel cutback (toe minus heel) = 4.500"
  • Hole length on the header = 4.500 ÷ 0.7071 = 6.364"
  • Centerline crosses the header top 3.3125 ÷ 1 = 3.313" from the work point
  • Station spacing for 16 stations = π × 4.500 ÷ 16 = 0.884" (about 7/8")

Step by step

  1. Mark the toe on the branch and draw a square line around the pipe through it.
  2. Divide the circumference into 16 equal stations starting at the toe (wrap a strip of paper, mark its length, fold it into 16). Station 8 is the heel.
  3. At each station, measure the ordinate from the square line toward the end of the pipe and mark it. The heel ends up the longest point.
  4. Join the marks with a smooth curve (a wrap-around or flexible batten), cut and bevel.
  5. On the header, mark the work point and the point where the branch centerline crosses the top (3.313" from the WP in the example). The hole is centered there and runs 6.364" along the top, from 0.131" to 6.495" past the work point. Scribe the hole from the cut branch, or lay it out with header-side ordinates.
  6. Tack, check the angle with a protractor or framing square, then weld.

Common mistakes

  • Using the saddle formula. The 90° saddle ordinates are symmetrical; a lateral's are not. The extra r × cos φ × cos α term is what makes the heel long.
  • Measuring from the wrong side. φ is counted from the toe, the acute side. Start at the heel and the whole cut is backwards.
  • Forgetting the hole is longer than the pipe. At 45° the hole on the header is 1.414 × the branch OD, not the OD.
  • Mixing OD and ID. These ordinates use outside diameters, so the outside edge fits and you grind the inside to seat. If you lay out from the ID, use it for both pipes on the job.

Always check the fit before welding.
