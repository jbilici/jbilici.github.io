Every answer comes with a drawing of your fittings, labelled with your numbers, and it moves as you change them. You can check the job at a glance.

Questions

Fitters ask.

How do I calculate a rolling offset?

Find the true offset first: √(roll² + set²). Then multiply it by the travel constant for your fitting (1.414 for 45°) to get the travel. Cutr does both steps, subtracts your fitting take-outs and draws the result.

What is the 45 degree offset multiplier?

1.414 for the travel and 1.000 for the run. For 22½° fittings it is 2.613 and 2.414, for 60° it is 1.155 and 0.577.

What is the take-out of a long-radius 90 elbow?

For butt-weld LR 90s it is 1.5 × NPS from 1" up (ASME B16.9); ½" and ¾" are both 1.50". Cutr includes the full B16.9 table, plus socket-weld and threaded fittings (B16.11).

Does Cutr include flanges and valves?

Yes: weld-neck, slip-on and socket-weld flanges in class 150, 300 and 600 (ASME B16.5), valve face-to-face lengths (B16.10), gaskets and stud-bolt lengths, count and wrench size. The flanges calculator gives the cut for a flange to a fitting, or both pipe pieces around a valve.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Everything is calculated on your phone, with no account, no login and no internet needed.

Is it a subscription?

No. Offsets (with cut length and take-outs), the take-out tables and the cut-length calculator are free for good. One in-app purchase unlocks rolling offsets, equal spread, flanges and valves, stud bolts, miters and saddles, with free updates. No subscription, no ads, no account.

Can I use feet and inches or metric?

Both. Type 3' 7 5/16" the way you'd say it, or work in millimetres, and round to 1/16" or 1/32".

How accurate is it?

The formulas come from trade-school references and the fitting tables are cross-checked against manufacturer catalogs. Every result shows the formula and the inputs it used. Always verify before you cut.
