Best pipefitter calculator apps for iPhone (2026 comparison)

An honest side-by-side of the iPhone pipefitter apps people actually use: Pipe Trades Pro, Pipe Calc Elite, PipeBook, Pipe Fitting, Piping DataBase XTREME, Pipedata, LMF Pipe Fitter Calculator and Cutr. Price model, what each does, last update, and who it suits.

The short answer

  • Most features, and you don't mind a subscription: Pipe Trades Pro Calc. It is the category leader by a wide margin (4,373 ratings) and adds flow, pressure-loss and pipe-data tools.
  • A Pipe Trades Pro-style calculator with one payment: Pipe Calc Elite ($19.99 once, updated October 2026).
  • One payment, reference tables plus field calculators: PipeBook ($12.99 once, new in 2026).
  • Free for offsets and take-outs, pay once for the rest: Cutr (free; Pro $6.99 once; not yet released).
  • Flange, fitting and valve dimensions as a reference book: Piping DataBase XTREME or Pipedata, knowing that XTREME has not been updated since 2015.

What each app does

Pipe Trades Pro Calc

The app version of Calculated Industries' Pipe Trades Pro calculator. Its listing covers cut lengths, take-outs, odd-degree fittings, offsets and rolling offsets, parallel pipe cutbacks, pipe areas and fill weights, flow, velocity and pressure loss, and pipe data up to 42" in several materials. It runs offline, on iPhone, iPad and Apple-silicon Macs. It is the most reviewed app in the category by far. The catch for many fitters is the price model: after the free trial it is an auto-renewing subscription ($4.99 a month or $39.99 a year on the US store). Suits: journeymen who already know the Pipe Trades Pro keystrokes, or anyone who wants flow and pressure tools in the same app.

Pipe Calc Elite

A one-purchase ($19.99) pipe trades calculator that has been on the App Store since 2013 and was updated in October 2026. Its listing covers simple and rolling offsets, take-outs and cut lengths for butt-weld and threaded elbows, thread make-up, pipe data for seven materials from 1/8" to 42", Darcy-Weisbach pressure loss, and a feet-inch-fraction calculator with a built-in manual. It states it is not affiliated with Calculated Industries. Suits: people who want a keypad calculator in the Pipe Trades Pro style without a subscription.

PipeBook Pipefitter Calculator

New in August 2026, one purchase ($12.99), no account. It combines reference tables (pipe schedules, B16.9 fitting take-offs, B16.5 flanges with stud bolts and ring gaskets) with calculators for rolling offsets with cut length, cutting a fitting from a 90, miters, parallel offsets, right angles and weight. Its listing says each table value is checked against several published sources. Suits: fitters who want tables and calculators in one paid app.

Pipe Fitting (xNeat.com)

Free to download with in-app purchases. Its listing covers basic, rolling and parallel (equal-spread) offsets, X° + Y° and 90° + X° back-to-back fittings, a miter cut for making custom angles from 90s, and a triangle solver, in inches or centimeters. Last updated April 2025. Suits: a quick offset calculator if you are happy with its in-app purchase terms.

Piping DataBase - XTREME

A paid ($9.99) dimension reference: elbows, tees, reducers, flanges (all classes, RF and RTJ) with bolt data, valves, olets, line blanks, gaskets and pipe schedules. It is a lookup book, not a calculator, and its last update was in April 2015. Suits: looking up dimensions for drawings and take-offs.

Pipedata

Free reference for dimensions and weights of 72 common ASME piping components, including valve and flange weights, in metric, US customary and fractions. Suits: a free dimensions and weights lookup.

Pipe Fitter Calculator (LMF.Services)

A $4.99 calculator for pipe, rolling and reducing-tee offsets, back-to-back and odd-angle fittings, with ANSI/ASME and DIN data. Last updated in November 2017, and its rating (3.6★) is the lowest here.

Cutr: Pipe Offset Calculator (ours)

Not released yet. You type measurements on a tape-measure keypad (inches and 1/16ths) and each answer comes with a drawing of your fittings, labelled with your numbers, and the formula it used.

  • Free, with no time limit: offsets for 11¼° to 60° or any angle, cut length with take-outs and root gaps, take-outs for butt-weld, socket-weld and threaded elbows, tees and reducers (ASME B16.9 and B16.11), and cut length between any two fittings.
  • Cutr Pro, $6.99 one-time: rolling offsets, equal-spread parallel offsets, flanges and valves in line (B16.5, B16.10), stud bolts, miters, saddle and branch templates, and laterals.
  • Offline, no account, no ads, no subscription.
  • What it doesn't do: flow and pressure loss, pipe weights, or a general feet-inch-fraction calculator. If you need those, Pipe Trades Pro or Pipe Calc Elite cover them.

Suits: apprentices and fitters who mostly need offsets, take-outs and cut lengths and want them free, and anyone who wants the Pro calculators without a subscription.

How to choose

  1. Count what you actually use. Most days are offsets, take-outs and cut lengths. Several apps above do those; you may not need the biggest one.
  2. Decide on the price model. Over three years a $39.99/year subscription costs about $120; the one-time apps here cost $6.99 to $19.99.
  3. Check the last update. Apps that haven't been updated in years can break on new iOS versions, and one wrong table value is expensive.
  4. Check its numbers against your book. Before you trust any app, run one offset and one take-out you already know (from the Blue Book, your fitting manufacturer's catalog or ASME B16.9) and compare.
