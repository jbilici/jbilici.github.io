How to lay out a pipe saddle (fish mouth) with ordinates

The ordinate method for cutting a branch pipe to fit a header at 90°: the formula, 16-station ordinate tables for 2" on 4" and 4" on 4", and step-by-step layout.

A saddle (or fish mouth) is the curved cut on the end of a branch pipe so it sits on a header at 90°. With the ordinate method you mark equal stations around the branch and measure a depth at each one.

The formula

For a branch of radius r on a header of radius R (use outside diameters ÷ 2), at station angle φ around the branch:

ordinate = R − √(R² − (r × sin φ)²)

  • Use 16 stations (every 22.5°) for most sizes, 32 for large pipe.
  • Station spacing on the wrap-around = π × branch OD ÷ number of stations.
  • The ordinate is 0 where the branch sits on top of the header (stations 0 and 8) and deepest at the sides (stations 4 and 12): R − √(R² − r²).

Stations 5–8 mirror 3–0, and 9–16 repeat 1–8. Spacing: 0.466" (about 7/16").

Ordinates: 4" on 4" (equal size), 16 stations

Station01234
Ordinate (in)00.1710.6591.3892.250

For equal sizes the deepest point equals the header radius. Spacing: 0.884" (about 7/8").

Step by step

  1. Square the branch end and draw a line around it.
  2. Divide the circumference into 16 equal stations (wrap a strip of paper, mark its length, fold it into 16).
  3. Number the stations so 0 and 8 will lie along the header's length.
  4. Measure each ordinate back from the line and mark it.
  5. Join the marks with a smooth curve (a flexible batten or wrap-around helps), cut, and grind to fit.

Outside or inside diameter?

Using the branch OD makes the outside edge fit snugly; you grind the inside to seat. Using the branch ID makes the inside edge seat and leaves the gap at the sides as the weld groove (2" Sch 40, ID 2.067, on 4": deepest ordinate 0.251" instead of 0.339"). Both are used; pick one per job and stay consistent.

Always check the fit before welding.
